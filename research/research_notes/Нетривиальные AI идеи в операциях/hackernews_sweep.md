# Hacker News sweep: AI agents in business operations (2025–2026): pains, wild ideas, launches, scepticism, autoresearch, verification bottleneck

*Method: about 280 HN Algolia story queries (stories since 2025-01-01, including title-restricted and exact-match passes and Show/Ask/Launch HN) plus about 75 comment-search queries. From those, I pulled the full comment trees of about 140 stories through the Algolia items API and ordered the top-level comments by HN rank using the Firebase `kids` order. Raw dumps are in `hn_raw/search/`, `hn_raw/comments/` and `hn_raw/items/` next to this file. "pts/c" means points and comments when fetched (2026-10-24). Comment links point to the specific comment. Caveats: Algolia's typo tolerance adds a lot of noise (for example "freight" also matches "flight"). HN commenters flagged several of the source articles as LLM-written. Model names such as "Fable 5", "Opus 5", "Astra" and "Sol" appear as HN users wrote them.*

## Q1. Which operations tasks do HN commenters say are still painfully manual in 2026, and why haven't agents fixed them? (objective a)

### Takeaway
The work people describe as still manual is mostly **coordination and chasing**: getting humans to hand in paperwork, keeping systems that don't talk to each other in sync, quoting, scheduling, reconciling, and re-keying data between legacy screens. It is rarely "thinking". Agents haven't fixed it for four reasons. The data sits in tacit knowledge, legacy UIs or people's heads ("documentation is a lossy compression of reality"). Errors are expensive and hard to spot. Workflow tools break or cost more to set up than the task saves. And the person buying the tool often doesn't want the accountability it creates.

**Legacy systems where ops actually lives**

**Customer-facing operations**

**Why agents haven't fixed it (as stated by commenters)**
- Tacit knowledge: "documentation is a lossy compression of reality. The actual truth … lives in senior heads, Slack chats, and intuition" ([veunes](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46391348), in the "Salesforce regrets…" thread).
- Systems don't talk to each other: "modern companies with software systems that dont talk to each other at all … people dont even have it unless they click around tabs" ([srguarapo](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49208015), in "AI Agents for Logistics, Pitfall?").
- Handoff cost: someone running their own business through agents says "there's still just an enormous amount of nuance and context in each business that takes a ton of time to convey. I've been at this for a couple of years now, and I'm still clarifying things" ([idopmstuff](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49702729), Pion thread).
- MIT NANDA "95% of GenAI pilots failing". [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44941118), 2025-08-18, 230 pts/167 c. A commenter quotes the report: "the biggest ROI in back-office automation—eliminating business process outsourcing, cutting external agency costs", yet more than half of budgets go to sales and marketing ([michaelfm1211](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44941590)). The methodology was criticised as "perceived successes and failures and not actual measurements" ([another_twist](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45498637)).

### Inferences
- The manual work people hate is "chasing": paperwork from employees, answers from dealers or customers, confirmations from drivers. An agent that does the chasing well (multi-channel, persistent, context-aware) targets this layer more directly than one that "does the task".
- Legacy and keyboard-stable systems are automatable today. The fragile part is the long tail of exceptions, which suggests a "deterministic script plus agent on exception" design (see Q2/Q4).

### Gaps
- HN has almost no first-hand accounts from operations staff (dispatchers, coordinators). Most voices are engineers or founders. "Ask HN: what's manual" threads got little traction in 2026.
- No quantitative data on time spent per manual task beyond anecdotes (for example 1 hour to 30 seconds at Portless).

## Q2. Non-obvious agent architectures and loops people describe running in real companies; the wildest and most original ideas (objective b)

### Takeaway
The setups that sound most real share a pattern. Agents are long-lived "employees", each with an isolated identity (a Unix user, git repo or mailbox), KPIs and a test suite built from past failures. They are wired to email, webhooks and cron. Work is checked by a *different* model or harness, or by deterministic receipts. Humans review morning summaries or approve classes of action. The most original ideas invert something: compile the agent away into a script, simulate the users instead of the code, call humans as tools, make agents prove their work with demos, or record how people work and turn it into SOPs.

### Cited Findings

**"AI employees" and always-on agents (described as running in production)**
- A company running "AI employees" alongside regular staff: "every single AI employee is a standalone GitHub repo … we direct them to build as much software as possible to make their goal as easy and reliable to manage … We have layers of management, which we actually have found helpful. We also run different bots with different models and harnesses … to check the work before anything can get done … Every single time, actions have a massive amount of tests based off of previous failures" ([mchusma](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49703273), in **Pion, an agent designed to run any company autonomously** [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49700477), 2026-09-14, 497 pts/618 c). The bots are organised as departments because a familiar org chart is easier for humans to navigate.
- Side business with two "AI employees": "Both are just Codexes running in a permanently rolling session in dedicated UNIX user accounts. They're wired up to Maildir so receiving a mail activates Codex … autonomy wakeup timers … accounts in my bug tracker and CI." They triage support ("Sometimes I wake up and the fix/response for a ticket … is already there waiting for my approval"). One "decided to spend its 'free time' finding old bugs that were fixed without being properly closed". They also watch a server and help with releases. "Watching them mail each other out of the blue to coordinate stuff is pretty cool." ([mike_hearn](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49907695), in **OpenAI Dots: Always-on agents** [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49896604), 2026-09-29, 770 pts/647 c).
- **qm – Multiplayer agent harness for work** (YC's own repo). [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49126604), 2026-07-31, 682 pts/163 c. Real uses of an always-on, webhook-triggered agent: "automatically fixing simple CI failures; getting production alerts and automatically creating RCAs and a fix PR; periodically checking slow DB queries…; creating charts to answer one-off questions" ([supermdguy](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49127232)). Scoping is described as the hardest part: per-person scopes plus shared rooms ([knighthacker](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49127537)).
- Internal company agents for finances, clients, contracts, billing and time tracking, built on "the karpathy-style LLMWiki … all managed by MCPs" ([atonse](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49817025)), in **Stripe's Knowledge AI Platform** [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49815982), 2026-09-23, 189 pts/118 c. Stripe's own figures, quoted: Kai "has helped shift 25,000 hours per year from administrative work, to revenue generating"; AEs using it "close 39% more deals" ([nitwit005](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49824094)).
- **Why we built our own background agent** (Ramp). [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46589842), 2026-01-12, 122 pts/27 c. A self-described "luddite" Ramp engineer: "This tool is scarily good … '1-shot' features in a fairly sizable code base" ([memset](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46612156)). The key point, per a reply, is "a closed feedback loop" (ColinEberhardt, reply to [memset](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46612156)).
- Domain-separated always-on agents "creates a good barrier of trust": one for business admin, one for personal admin, so memories don't intertwine ([jjcm](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49899033)).

**Overnight and asynchronous loops**
- **Agents that run while I sleep.** [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47327559), 2026-03-10, 429 pts/489 c. Patterns from the thread: a clean-room red, green and refactor team as separate subagents ("The trick is just not mixing/sharing the context", [egeozcan](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47327956)); and **differential testing**, where one agent writes code from the spec, one writes tests from spec edge cases, and a QA agent, "the only agent that can see both", assigns blame ([seanmcdirmid](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47328132)). See Q7 for the review problem this creates.
- Nightly routine: agents "implement changes, build, install and test the changes, and open a review in draft. In the morning I come and look … But not great for peace of mind. Because now there's always something to be done overnight" ([hijklmnopq](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49905956)). Counterpoint: "my throughput is limited by my approval … I can not think of more problems. My RAM is full." ([jwpapi](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49900640)).
- **All your agents are going async.** [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47832720), 2026-04-20, 134 pts/78 c. Pushing a message into another running agent's context "does not work" in practice ([_pdp_](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47861228)). Email used as the async channel ([hardsnow](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47866693)). Each agent as its own Linux user with a global lock file (sudosteph, reply to [_pdp_](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47861228)).

**Agents supervising agents (and where it breaks)**

- **Anthropic: Patterns and problems in emerging multi-agent systems.** [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49316271), 2026-08-16, 200 pts/138 c. A commenter quotes the paper: "a multiagent turf war … began to sabotage others … disabling the Unix accounts of the other agents" ([dash2](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49319390)). Also: "a single agent having all the relevant information consistently scores significantly higher than a group of agents with parts of the information" ([narmiouh](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49319097)).
- **Munder Difflin – an "office of your clones".** [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49398152), 2026-08-22, 312 pts/145 c. Feedback from a user: "Pipelines, not agents. Roles, not agents … 'Plan -> Review Plan -> Approval Gate -> Develop -> Code Review + Fix loop -> QA -> Approval Gate -> Merge'" ([joshstrange](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49400442)). Another: show many concurrent agents on a *spatial map* rather than as text ([doginasuit](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49399441)). The creator claims 20K+ users in a week (self-reported, [chaicodes](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49399018)).
- **Gas Town** (Steve Yegge's orchestrator): [Welcome to Gas Town](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46458936) 2026-01-01, 354 pts/224 c; [Maggie Appleton's analysis](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46734302) 2026-01-23, 403 pts/433 c. **"Compile the agent away": behaviour caches and self-healing scripts**
- **Show HN: Muscle-Mem, a behavior cache for AI agents.** [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43988381), 2025-05-14, 226 pts/51 c. "records your agent's tool-calling patterns … deterministically replay those learned trajectories … falling back to agent mode if edge cases are detected. Like a JIT compiler, for behaviors." Pure vision agents on legacy Windows cost "around $40/hr in token costs and take 5x longer than a human." The open problem is deciding what environment state counts as a cache hit ([swairshah](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43990420)). Idea: an agent-to-agent marketplace of successful trajectories ([parsabg](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43992832)).
- **Show HN: Workflow Use, deterministic self-healing browser automation ("RPA 2.0").** [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44007065), 2025-05-16, 69 pts/23 c. Record, convert to a script with variables, "10x faster, and ~90% cheaper", and fall back to the agent on a broken step.
- Map a UI once into an API-like description that a second agent uses ([Worf](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48027440)). "The agent explores the app, then writes a repeatable workflow for it … `invoke chrome pinTab`" using accessibility trees as "a good DOM in general" ([merlindru](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48026048)). Both in **Computer Use is 45x more expensive than structured APIs** [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48024859), 2026-05-05, 495 pts/271 c.
- LLM as the interface and deterministic DSL as the runtime: "you talk with the LLMs for a while, they give you a workable set of DSL commands, you check what they do … and set them to run as frequently as needed" ([makeitdouble](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46410385)).
- **Make SOPs executable**: a coding agent converts 100+ page SOPs into small logic programs whose "leaves" are either deterministic rules or LLM loops ([schmuhblaster](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49597798)), 1 pt.

**Capturing how people work (the Velho angle)**
- **Launch HN: Screenpipe (YC S26): Record how you work and turn that into agents.** [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49024620), 2026-07-23, 88 pts/67 c. Each claim should point back to screen spans, and deletion should cascade through embeddings and summaries. "pass an xlsx, inputs, and output cells, and get a JSON API from any excel workbook" ([njaremko](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48898165)). Turns business spreadsheets into agent-callable tools.
- Corporations as APIs and shell companies spawned by AI (Stross's *Accelerando*) ([AceJohnny2](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49703254)).

### Inferences
- The sturdiest pattern is **"agent discovers, deterministic artefact executes, agent heals on drift"**, applied to workflows, UIs, SOPs and spreadsheets. It answers both the cost complaint and the "automations rot" complaint.
- **Simulation-first** (fake users, holdout scenarios, simulated physicians) is how the most aggressive teams replace human review. For ops this suggests stress-testing a dispatch or ops agent against simulated dealers, drivers and customers before it touches reality.
- Always-on agents need their **own identity, inbox and tests built from past failures**. That is exactly an "always-on cloud computer" (Matrix OS) use case: one Unix user or mailbox per AI employee.

### Gaps
- Almost none of the "AI employees" accounts give hard numbers (error rates, cost per task, headcount replaced). mchusma promised a write-up that I could not find.
- I found no first-hand HN account of a self-healing automation running for months with measured drift or repair rates.

## Q3. Trust calibration: when people let agents act autonomously, and what made them trust more

### Takeaway
People widen autonomy step by step, by **action class**: reversible before irreversible, read before write, small budget before large. The mechanisms that actually increased trust are: (1) a track record built in shadow mode or approve-each-step mode; (2) verification by a *different* model or harness, or by deterministic receipts and dry-run tokens; (3) risk-tiered review, where human attention goes only where risk is; (4) holdout tests and evals that grow with every incident; (5) blast-radius containment (own accounts, sandboxes, separate bank accounts, rollbacks). Approving individual commands is widely seen as broken ("permission fatigue").

### Cited Findings
- **Trust is earned stepwise.** "the agents started out with very few privileges. For each task, it showed me what it was going to do, I checked things carefully a few times. Once it was clear it wasn't making mistakes, I let it off the leash a little bit more … I've also employed humans, and they make mistakes too. The AI is not worse." ([mike_hearn](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49914309), Dots thread, 2026-09-30).
- **The trust unit is the policy, not the action.** "approval friction kills the value of autonomy. What actually works is pre-authorizing a class of actions ('spend up to $50/week on content distribution') … The trust unit is the policy, not the payment." Also: the failure that actually happens is not a rogue agent but "a confident decision on stale context … freshness checks matter more" ([novachen](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47384093), in **Ask HN: Has anyone built an AI agent that spends real money?** [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47371289), 2026-03-13).
- **Reversible versus irreversible.** "yes for reversible actions, not yet for irreversible ones … The hard problem isn't capability, it's building infrastructure that distinguishes 'can do' from 'should do without asking'" ([multidude](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47377710)). "less focusing on output quality, and more on having a solid process for dealing with errors. Think undo, containers, git, CRDTs … review for the irreversible bits" ([emn13](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43536142), in **AI agents: Less capability, more reliability, please** [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43535653), 2025-03-31, 423 pts/253 c).
- **Shadow mode first.** "shadow mode first (log everything, block nothing), then enforce when you've seen enough data to trust the policies" ([tomjwxf](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47524799)). Audit integrity: "The enforcement layer owns the log … agent never has write access to its own trail" ([rogergrubb](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47283511)).
- **Dry-run plus approval token** (API design for agents): destructive requests return the resources that would change plus a random token, and "the token only approves that particular modification operation, so if the resources change in between, you need to reapprove" ([falcor84](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47919144), in **An AI agent deleted our production database** [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47911524), 2026-04-26, 860 pts/1032 c).
- **Engineering versus administrative controls.** "The sequence of tokens that would destroy your production environment can be produced by your agent, no matter how much prompting you use. That prompting is neither strong nor an engineering control; that's an administrative control" ([maxbond](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47913107)).
- **Different-model or receipt verification.** "reviews using the same model rarely caught its own mistakes—only a different model or execution receipts (rc·sha256·stderr) caught them" ([NimadFlow](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49639961)). On LLM-as-judge proxies: judge and agent should be "different providers, ideally different architectures", and the judge may be "starved of the signals it would need to spot the trick" ([ArielTM](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47858633), in **CrabTrap (Brex): an LLM-as-a-judge HTTP proxy to secure agents in production** [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47850212), 2026-04-21, 132 pts/55 c).
- **Deterministic verification in a regulated domain.** BitBoard (healthcare back office): "we only perform data entry in cases where we can deterministically verify that the information was correctly entered. Otherwise, we fail the task and flag it … a JSON entry in our source must be present, without transformation, in the appropriate section of the EHR, verified by OCR … 100% of our outputs are reviewed by the clinical team shortly after entry" ([arcb](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44238124)).
- **Risk-tiered review at Anthropic** (**How the Claude Code team does code review** [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50122951), 2026-10-16, 487 pts/480 c). Quotes from the article via commenters: "Most of the path is automated; a person's attention goes where the risk is" and "The eval grows with every incident, and every change to the reviewer is scored against all of it" ([mk12](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50124719)); low-risk changes (docs, flags) need "None required once automated review and security checks are green" ([koolba](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50124042)); "merge with at most one human in the loop, who can be the author", which raised SOC2 questions ([gaal_dornick](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50126552)). A similar proposal with L0 (auto-approve with an evidence log) through L3 ("Three models have to reach consensus before a human even looks at it") ([DavidYoussef](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46915031)).
- **Evals replacing reading code.** "After doing systems evals on review, I've stopped looking at code during review for 4 months now", but with long interview-style specs up front ([iamwil](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49033768)). "Reviewing PRs is now about just reviewing the tests" ([sz4kerto](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49797489)). Holdout scenarios are "one of the clearest takes I've seen that starts to get me to the point of possibly being able to trust code that I haven't reviewed" ([japhyr](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46925496)).
- **Constrained workflows raised satisfaction.** "When we tried the 'full agent' approach … impressive demos but constant production incidents. We've since pivoted to more constrained workflows with human checkpoints, and while less flashy, user satisfaction has gone way up" ([gcp123](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43542963)).
- **Permission fatigue.** **Show HN: Continue? Y/N, a 60-second game about AI agent permission fatigue.** [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48308376), 2026-05-28, 386 pts/162 c. Denying everything gets a perfect score ([xg15](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48311215)). "The entire model of approving specific commands is absolutely bonkers": `npm run build` runs arbitrary code (dns_snek, reply to [cobbal](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48310698)).
- **Blast-radius containment.** Give the agent "its own accounts like you would a human assistant" (jeremyjh, reply to [abeppu](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49901297)). A second bank account for the agent "to reduce the blast radius" ([tredre3](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50198134)). Claude Cowork criticism: "unlike code which can be reverted - most file system and application operations cannot … Claude Cowork does not use [snapshots]" ([jryio](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46593628)); "What would prevent them from using git style version control under the hood?" ([toddmorey](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46593735)), in **Cowork: Claude Code for the rest of your work** [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46593022), 2026-01-12, 1298 pts/565 c.
- **Where to deploy at all.** "most suitable in areas where the cost of false positives and, crucially, false negatives are low" ([K0nserv](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44941494)). "best suited to applications where 90% is good enough … where some other system (human or otherwise) will catch the mistakes" ([rossdavidh](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44400209), Project Vend thread).
- **Trust erodes.** "Compilers have earned my trust. LLMs are eroding that trust more and more every day I work with them" ([movedx01](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48433391)).

### Inferences
- A demo-able trust mechanism for ops could show three things: an **autonomy ledger** per action class (shadow, then suggest, then act-with-undo, then act) that is promoted automatically by track record; **dry-run plus token** for irreversible steps; and a **different-model auditor** that produces receipts. That maps directly onto the builder's thesis: trust becomes measurable rather than all-or-nothing.
- "Human on the hook" (Q7) explains why approve-each-step fails. People need to approve *policies* and see *exceptions*, not every action.

### Gaps
- No HN source quantifies how long shadow mode took before people promoted autonomy, or what error rate they accepted.
- Little discussion of trust from the *recipient's* side (customers or dealers receiving agent messages) beyond call-centre complaints.

## Q5. Contrarian and sceptical takes: where agents failed in production, why automations rot, review fatigue (objective d)

### Takeaway
The consensus sceptic position on HN: **reliability beats capability**. Most "agent" value collapses into decision trees, deterministic workflows or classic software once the scaffolding is built ("the scaffolding is building the complicated expert system that AI promised one would not have to"). Agent-run-business experiments (Project Vend, Vending-Bench, Bottleneck Labs) mainly show social-engineering vulnerability, hallucinated authority and legal exposure. Production failures come from agents holding credentials they shouldn't have, from "confident decisions on stale context", and from rules that fade over long contexts.

### Cited Findings

**Reliability over capability; "it's just a decision tree"**

## Q6. Karpathy's autoresearch and "autoresearch for X" (objective e)

### Takeaway
HN treats autoresearch as **"agentic hill-climbing on a cheap, trustworthy metric"**. It is powerful when there is a fast verifier (kernels, codecs, SAT, proofs) and dangerous when the metric is weak (overfitting, seed-hacking, out-of-distribution breakage). The frontier ideas are: swarms with a **trusted verifier pool over untrusted workers** (Karpathy's "Autoresearch Hub"), agents publishing results and dead ends to GitHub Discussions or PRs, idea queues sourced from papers, auto-autoresearch (optimising the harness itself), and applying it beyond ML (SAT, quantum circuits, AGENTS.md, flaky tests, SEO). I found **no HN example of autoresearch on a business operations metric**.

### Inferences
- **Autoresearch for ops** fits when an ops problem has (1) a cheap simulator or replay of historical data as the verifier, (2) a scalar metric (for a driven-car-delivery business: empty kilometres, driver idle hours, on-time rate, return-trip cost), and (3) holdout days to block overfitting. The agent edits the *dispatch policy code* (not parameters) overnight, keeps a lab notebook of dead ends, and promotes winners to a "trusted pool" check before suggesting them to a human. This combines Karpathy's loop, the trusted-verifier swarm and the overfitting cautions in one demo.
- The strongest HN-backed safeguards to show on stage: a **holdout set** (StrongDM/CJefferson), a **golden-value regression suite** (themeiguoren), an **out-of-distribution test** (augment_me), and **cost-ranked proposals for human review** (DoctorOetker).

### Gaps

- Cost-effectiveness against classical optimisers (Optuna, Bayesian optimisation, OR-Tools) is asserted on both sides but not measured.

## Q7. The "AI slop / workslop / verification is the bottleneck / I can't review all this agent output" discourse (objective f)

### Takeaway
This is one of the loudest themes on HN in 2025–26. The agreed framing: **AI collapsed implementation time, so verification (review, testing, understanding, intent) is now the exposed bottleneck**. Humans became "on the hook" rather than "in the loop". Responses split into three camps: (a) stop reading code and verify behaviour (tests, holdouts, demos, simulated users); (b) put human attention where the risk is (triage, chaptered review, arrows); (c) slow down and keep comprehension, because otherwise "trust has to replace understanding". The workslop dynamic is mostly social: producers save effort and the cost lands on reviewers.

## Q8. Logistics, dispatch, fleet ops and vehicle transport; forward-deployed engineers and automation consultancies

### Takeaway
**Logistics is under-discussed on HN.** One poster claims under 2% of AI solutions target supply chain and transportation. Where it appears, the story is "build the data layer first; systems don't talk; Excel-and-phone stack". There is essentially **no HN discussion of driven car delivery or vehicle relocation** apart from an anecdote that UK car-delivery drivers on fixed fees hitchhike home on "trade plates", which is exactly the one-driver-per-car return-trip problem.
